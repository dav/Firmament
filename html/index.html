The app

An enormous, perfectly regular lattice — fixed in space.

Dav’s Celestial Firmament threads the Earth’s orbital path through the solar system with a lattice of markers, centered on the Sun and fixed in space. It draws the ones above your horizon over the live camera view of the sky, exactly where they are.

The lattice never moves. You do. Earth spins once a day and races along its orbit at about 30 km/s, so the grid is constantly sweeping past you. Now, for the first time, you can watch that motion happen. A solar ephemeris accurate to about an arcminute computes your position relative to the Sun — the lattice origin — from your GPS coordinates and the current time.

Three layouts

The same idea, from three distances.

Grid

Fills space with a regular lattice of cubes. The plainest statement of the thing: markers everywhere, evenly spaced, going past.

Tube

Floats Earth inside a ring-lined bore that follows its orbit around the Sun — the track from the story, drawn where it actually runs.

Ride

Threads that same tunnel through you as the center, rings arching overhead just tens of kilometers up. This is the one that gets people.

A narrated intro tour explains it all on first launch, and a picture-in-picture orrery keeps you oriented — showing Earth from afar, moving through the very nodes hanging in your sky.
